# Voice Playbook — How Ethan's LinkedIn posts must sound

This is the bible. Every draft is judged against it.

## The non-negotiables

1. **Sounds like a real person, not a headline.** If you can read the opening line out loud and it feels like a human said it, good. If it feels like a Forbes subhead, an AI summary, or a brand account caption, kill it. Contractions are good. Sentence fragments are good. Smooth corporate cadence is the enemy.
2. **Strong hook at the start.** First line, under 200 characters (LinkedIn truncates at ~210). It must do *one* of: take a side, drop a specific number/observation, or name a problem the reader feels today. If the post would still make sense with the first line deleted, the hook is too soft.
3. **Every post provides value.** A reader walks away with a useful frame, an insight from inside the work, a tactic to try, or a pattern they recognize. If the only takeaway is "Ethan has opinions," it's not done. Test: would a stranger *save* this post?
4. **Specific > clever.** Real numbers, real brand names, real conversations beat metaphors and abstractions.
5. **One idea per post.** Resist the urge to bundle. If two ideas, two posts.

## The three hook types that work in 2026

Pick one — never mix:

- **Bold / contrarian statement.** "Most brands hiring UGC creators are wasting money on the part that doesn't matter." Punchy, takes a side.
- **Unexpected stat or specific.** "I shot 47 product videos last quarter. The two that converted best had no script at all." Concrete, surprising, makes you want the story.
- **High-stakes problem the audience faces today.** "The reason your last UGC batch flopped isn't the creator — it's the brief." Diagnoses a pain they recognize.

## Structure

```
[Hook line — under 200 chars, lands the punch alone]

[1-line space]

[Setup — 1-3 short lines. Concrete. What happened, what you saw.]

[Turn — the insight or the twist. The "here's what most people miss" beat.]

[Payoff — what the reader should now do, think, or save.]

[Optional one-line kicker. Re-affirms the thesis in fewer words than the hook.]
```

Short paragraphs. Often one sentence per line. White space is a feature, not a bug.

## Voice tics to use (Ethan's own)

- Italics on the load-bearing word: "*actually* converts", "*real* customers"
- Contrast pairs: "Stiff script. Real conversation."
- "No X, no Y" rhythm: "No fake energy, no rented voice, no script."
- Lowercase casual asides where it fits — but don't force it

## Banned phrases (instant rewrite)

- "In today's fast-paced world"
- "Game-changer", "unlock", "leverage", "synergy"
- "Here's the thing..." (cliché now)
- "Let that sink in"
- "Hot take:" (overused; if it's a hot take, just say it)
- Em-dashes used for drama back-to-back
- "Folks", "y'all" if it's not how he actually talks
- Anything starting with "🚨" or excessive emoji
- Three-word punchy lines stacked for fake gravitas: "Strategy.\nExecution.\nResults."
- "What I learned from..."  (overdone)
- Fake-humble flexes ("I almost didn't post this but...")

## Rules for tying a news story to a UGC creator POV

The whole point of the agent is: news happens → Ethan posts something *only he can credibly post about it*. Translation rules:

- **Macro news → micro creator economics.** A Fed rate decision becomes "here's why brand UGC budgets just got tighter." A Big Tech earnings story becomes "what AI spending means for the people actually shooting the ads."
- **Lead with what you saw firsthand.** If the news is the topic, Ethan's lived experience is the angle. Never summarize the news — react to it from inside the work.
- **Avoid hot-takes on industries he doesn't operate in.** Oil prices, sports business, geopolitics — he can connect them to creator/brand work, but he can't pretend to be an expert. The bridge has to be honest: "I don't know the oil market, but I do know what happens to creator budgets when fuel goes up — small DTC brands cut creative first."

## What to do when none of the headlines fit

It's fine to say "today's news isn't a fit." A weak forced post is worse than no post.
